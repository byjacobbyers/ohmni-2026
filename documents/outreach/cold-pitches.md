# Cold pitches, 20 variants

Drafted from the Ohmni deck (/present/ohmni-system) for review. One hook per
pitch, none repeats the "AI spins up pages faster than the stack" example.
Rules held: plain declarative voice, no em or en dashes, pain before product,
soft CTAs, no invented statistics. The only study cited is HBR 2011 (2,241
companies; responses inside an hour were 60x more likely to qualify the lead
than responses past 24 hours). Cost figures match the deck. The Spanish site,
the A/B experiment, and the Markdown/llms.txt layer are live and checkable.

Swap CTAs freely between pitches. FirstName and CompanyName are merge fields.

## 2. The mailroom with no manager

Hi FirstName,

I describe marketing stacks to non-engineers as a mailroom: a front desk that takes the envelope, workers that file it, and a manager who makes sure nothing gets dropped.

Most stacks I audit have plenty of workers. CRM, analytics, email, Slack, all watching the same forms. What they never have is the manager. Nothing queues, nothing retries, nothing keeps a logbook, so a dropped lead looks exactly like a delivered one.

I install the manager. If you have ever wondered whether a lead quietly vanished, you probably need one. Open to a short call?

Thanks,
Jacob

## 3. The thank-you page that lies

Hi FirstName,

That is what silent failure looks like, and most stacks are full of it, because nobody gets an alert when a workflow between two tools stops working.

I build lead paths where a failure pages a human instead of hiding. Want me to check whether your forms are telling you the truth? Takes me about a day, costs you nothing.

## 10. Design that never finishes

Hi FirstName,

Hourly design work never finishes, because finishing is not in its incentives. Every landing page becomes a new negotiation about buttons that were already decided three pages ago.

The fix is boring and structural: make every design decision once, store it as code, and let new pages inherit it. I call it a design dictionary. After it exists, "we need a new page" stops being a design project.

I wrote up how this plays out, and I see the symptom on most sites I audit: count your button styles. If it is more than three, worth a chat?

Jacob
