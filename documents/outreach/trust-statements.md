# Trust statements, 10 variants

For touch two in the outreach sequence: the opener named the pain, this one
answers "why believe you." Rules: every claim is verifiable by the prospect
or already published, because we have no client logos to borrow trust from,
and checkable proof beats borrowed proof anyway. No em dashes, no invented
numbers. Each works as a standalone follow-up message ("Hi FirstName," +
statement + soft close like "Happy to show you." or "No reply needed, just
context.") or as a line inside a longer message.

---

### 1. I eat my own cooking

Everything I pitch runs on my own site first. The lead pipeline, the analytics, the A/B test on the homepage, the Spanish version, all live on ohmni.tech right now. I never sell a client something I have not already run in production myself.

*Use when: the opener was any capability pitch. Strongest general-purpose statement.*

### 2. The code is public

My site's entire codebase is public on GitHub. You can read exactly how I build before we ever talk, and any developer you trust can audit it. I work in the open because the work holds up.

*Use when: the prospect has a technical evaluator. Verifiable in one click.*

### 3. Fire me tomorrow

Every account, every line of code, every integration I build is in your name from day one. You could end the engagement tomorrow and keep everything working. I am the only vendor you will talk to this month whose architecture assumes you might leave.

*Use when: the opener was about agencies, lock-in, or exit costs.*

### 4. The no-pitch audit promise is published

My free audit ends with findings, not a retainer pitch. That promise is written on the audit page itself, publicly, where breaking it would cost me every future prospect who reads it. I made it expensive for myself to be pushy.

*Use when: the opener offered the audit and got silence.*

### 5. The punch list says "your team can fix this"

When my audit finds something your own team or current agency can fix, the punch list says so. It costs me work to write that line, which is exactly why you can believe the lines that say otherwise.

*Use when: prospect likely suspects the audit is a sales funnel.*

### 6. A decade in the seat next to yours

I have spent ten years as the technical right hand to marketing leaders, usually deep inside one system for years at a time. I am not an agency discovering your stack on your dime. This is the only job I do.

*Use when: seniority or "who even are you" is the doubt.*

### 7. One person, built for the bus

The fair question about a one-person operation is what happens if I get hit by a bus. So everything is documented and any competent React developer can pick it up. The honest answer to the risk is the architecture, not a promise.

*Use when: the prospect went quiet after learning it is one person.*

### 8. I will tell you what to ignore

Every audit includes a section on what looks broken but is not worth your money right now. Anyone can hand you a list of problems. Knowing which ones to skip is the part you cannot get from a tool.

*Use when: the prospect has been burned by audit-shaped sales documents.*

### 9. Numbers I can defend

I cite my sources. The response-time statistic in my first message is from a Harvard Business Review audit of 2,241 companies, and every number on my site traces to something you can check. If I cannot verify a claim, I do not make it.

*Use when: following up the speed-to-lead opener specifically.*

### 10. Tools at cost, no markup

Every tool in your stack gets billed to you directly at cost. I take no reseller margin, so I have no reason to recommend software you do not need. My only incentive is that the system works.

*Use when: the opener was about suite costs or tool sprawl.*

## Assembly note

A touch-two message is one statement plus a two-line frame, for example:

Hi FirstName,

No pitch in this one, just context on who is writing. Everything I sell runs on my own site first: the lead pipeline, the live A/B test, the Spanish version, all on ohmni.tech right now. The code is public on GitHub.

If the timing is wrong, no worries at all.
