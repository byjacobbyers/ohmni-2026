What I found on deepgram.com

Hosting

deepgram.com resolves to Netlify; the docs run on Vercel. The posting says Vercel in production. A platform move and internationalization are the same project, so decide the host first.

Locale

No hreflang and no locale segment anywhere in the sitemap. Locale routing is greenfield, the one thing you never want to build twice.

AI readability

Docs: a 401 line llms.txt, .md from any page, a hosted MCP server. Marketing: 59 lines, .md returns 404, no JSON-LD on the homepage, no AI crawler decision in robots.txt.

The lead path

54 third-party hosts on the homepage, four session recorders at once, four tools watching the same forms. Everything runs in the visitor's browser; nothing queues, retries or keeps a logbook.

Proof, live on ohmni.tech today

Internationalization

ohmni.tech/es: locale routing, reciprocal hreflang with x-default, per-URL sitemap alternates, Accept-Language suggested once and never forced, English fallback with a canonical. A translate button in Sanity; 18 documents in one evening.

A manager in the mailroom

Every form submit goes through a queue (Inngest) with retries and a logbook before five workers run: CRM, email, server-side analytics, notification, alert. A skipped job never looks like a done job.

Design system as the build

Tokens as data, a component library that cannot drift, templates marketers compose in the CMS. The full deck for this role is assembled from that library with no developer.

A/B testing, running now

One experiment live on the homepage: a sticky cookie assigned at the edge, variants are ordinary Sanity pages served by rewrite, the variant rides on every PostHog event and on the server-side lead. Start or stop from a document, no deploy.

A first ninety days, sequenced so nothing gets built twice
Weeks 1 to 2

Decide the ground

Hosting decided once. Audit the repo, the Sanity schema, the tag stack. Marketing sees a preview link for every change.

Weeks 3 to 6

Make the site readable

JSON-LD from Sanity, llms.txt and .md from the CMS, an explicit robots.txt decision. Marketing sees pages assistants can quote.

Weeks 7 to 10

A manager in the mailroom

Server-side events, a queue with retries and a logbook, alerts for silent failure. A lead is never silently lost again.

Weeks 11 to 13

Lay the i18n foundation

Locale routing, locale-scoped Sanity, hreflang with x-default, a translation workflow the content team can live in.

The QA program is not a phase. Content validation rules so bad content cannot publish, and a smoke suite on the conversion paths, from week one. It is what lets the other phases ship. One honest gap: a one-person repo is not a repo with agency contributors, stakeholders and a QA program. That is the thing I want next.
