## Why Ohmni exists

Marketing teams get sold tools. Nobody sells them the wiring between the tools. The website is a project, the CRM is a different vendor, analytics is whoever set it up three years ago, and email is a platform someone signed for. Each piece works on its own. The seams between them are where campaigns die. And each vendor is responsible only for their slice, so when a campaign dies between two of them, it is genuinely no one's fault. That is the problem.

**Say:** "We run the whole system rather than one slice of it, because every problem worth fixing sits between two vendors."

## Six versions of the same button

Three designers over four years, each adding one small adjustment. None of them wrong on their own.

**Say:** "At Craft.co it was four competing design systems running at once. Consolidating them into one was the highest-leverage thing we did that year."

## Every design decision made once

Four layers, each built from the one below. Change a token and every page above it updates, without anyone opening a page to edit it.

**Say:** "That is the difference between a design system and a style guide. One is enforced by the code. The other is a document people are asked to remember."

## The average company takes 42 hours to answer a lead

Harvard Business Review submitted a test lead to 2,241 US companies and timed the reply.

**Say:** "Nothing errored. Nothing alerted anyone. Every one of those companies believed follow-up was working."

## The buyer has a reply in under a minute

The submission is stored before any integration runs. Everything after it is queued work that retries, so a vendor outage delays follow-up instead of losing it.

**Say:** "Every step retries, and a permanent failure alerts a human. Silent failure is the expensive kind."

## Half of B2B buyers now start with a chatbot

Half of B2B software buyers now open an assistant instead of a search engine, and most assistants do not execute JavaScript.

**Say:** "You are not ranked lower. You are absent from the answer entirely."

## Be the source AI models cite

A person sees a normal page and the engine receives an empty one. Where your structured data is added decides which of those happens.

**Say:** "View source on any site and search for ld+json. If it only appears in the browser, that site is invisible to a large share of AI crawlers." (Offer to do it live on their site.)

## About a third of your visitors were never counted

Roughly a third of US internet users run an ad blocker, and the standard filter lists block Google Analytics by default.

**Say:** "This is why two dashboards disagree, and why the finance team quietly stopped believing either one."

## Numbers that survive an ad blocker and a CFO

The gap is rarely configuration. The request goes to a tracking domain the browser refuses to load, so a share of visitors never reach the counter at all.

**Say:** "Expect traffic to rise and conversion rate to fall the month this lands. Nothing changed except that you started counting everyone."

## Four problems, one cause

These are not four services. They are four symptoms of a stack assembled by different people at different times.

**Say:** "One operator across the whole stack. No agency handoffs, no vendor gaps."

## How we solve this

Migration is part of the engagement, not a separate build fee. Deliverables are work you ask for, counted against the allowance. Upkeep is work you never ask for, and it never touches the allowance: security updates, redirect checks, tracking that drifted after someone added a tag, performance regressions.

**Say:** "You get moved onto the system once, then it is kept standing and extended from there."

## What the alternatives cost

Marketing Hub Enterprise is about $3,600 a month, roughly $43k a year, plus $6,000 onboarding; Professional lands nearer $17k. The agency stack runs $102k to $270k a year and each vendor owns a slice. The in-house hire is $125k to $170k loaded, software on top, one skill set, and most cannot ship production code.

**Say:** "Gartner puts martech utilization at 49 percent. About half of that spend produces no active output."

## Pricing

Foundation $5k: migrate, then two improvements a month, monthly readout. Growth Engine $8k: four deliverables, biweekly. Pipeline Partner $12k: six deliverables, weekly working session; one loaded ops hire costs more and cannot ship code.

**Say:** "Every month ends with one concrete improvement, shipped. Six month minimum, then month to month."
